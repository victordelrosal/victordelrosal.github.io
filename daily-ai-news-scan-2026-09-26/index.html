Meta launches Muse, an AI agent app that became top free iPhone app in US

Daily AI News Scan — Saturday, September 26, 2026

Executive Summary

Meta's Muse AI agent app rocketed to the top of the US App Store, signaling that consumer demand for task-completing AI agents has crossed a mainstream threshold. Meanwhile, Microsoft consolidated its AI offerings into an all-in-one Copilot super app, intensifying competition across the agent landscape. A quieter but structurally significant concern: researchers and critics are raising alarms that LLMs, by normalizing statistical distributions, may be quietly eroding originality and innovation across creative and professional fields.

Top Stories

1. Meta launches Muse, an AI agent app that became top free iPhone app in US

Meta's Muse, a digital butler AI agent capable of completing real-world transactions, debuted as the top free iPhone app in the US, with Walmart, Best Buy, Sephora, Expedia, and Shopify already signed as partners — Meta earns transaction fees on each agent-completed purchase. Amazon notably blocked Muse from its platform, a telling move given that AI agents optimizing for best prices directly threaten the company's $68 billion annual advertising revenue from sponsored listings. Under the hood, Muse isolates each user in a private virtual machine and runs sub-agents in parallel to address latency, though Meta acknowledged the model draws heavily from the open-source OpenClaw project, contradicting earlier claims it was built entirely in-house. The rapid ascent of Muse, alongside competing platforms Instinct, OpenClaw, and Claude, marks a decisive moment in the agent wars: the battleground is now retail commerce, and the friction point is who controls the purchase path.

Source: Wired AI

2. Microsoft unveils Copilot super app

Microsoft launched a consolidated Copilot application combining AI chat, coding assistants, autonomous agents, and Office productivity tools in a single interface, with support for models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Microsoft itself. The move positions Copilot as a platform rather than a feature, directly competing with Meta's Muse in the emerging AI agent ecosystem. The launch comes amid broader geopolitical turbulence: the US government has reportedly asked OpenAI and Anthropic to withhold their newest models from the UK's AI Security Institute pending domestic testing, and ByteDance's Singapore subsidiary was found routing 2,304 Nvidia B200 chips through a Norwegian data center to circumvent US export controls. The Justice Department also entered the AI legal landscape, arguing that AI training can qualify as transformative fair use — a position with significant implications for ongoing copyright litigation.

Source: TechCrunch AI

3. LLMs as Standardizing Forces Threaten Originality and Innovation

Critics including Erik Hoel and Azeem Azhar are advancing the argument that large language models, trained on incentive-shaped data distributions, function as powerful homogenizing forces — statistically averaging output across creative and professional fields and marginalizing outliers. The analogy drawn is to MFA creative writing programs, which critics argue flattened literary fiction by normalizing a particular voice and structure across a generation of writers. If the same dynamic plays out at the scale LLMs operate, the implications extend well beyond literature into scientific research, product design, and strategic thinking. The concern is not that AI produces bad output, but that it may systematically suppress the kind of deviant, unconventional thinking that drives genuine innovation.

Source: The Rundown AI
