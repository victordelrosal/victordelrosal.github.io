OpenAI Contractors Read Real ChatGPT Conversations

Daily AI News Scan — Wednesday, September 16, 2026

Executive Summary

OpenAI's Project Lily reveals the company is paying contractors to read and rate real user conversations without clear disclosure, raising significant privacy concerns for enterprise and consumer users alike. Meanwhile, Codex has quietly displaced traditional IDEs across OpenAI's internal operations, with 90% non-engineering adoption signaling a broader industry shift in how software gets built. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has published a concrete governance proposal calling for embedded third-party AI evaluators and eventual international development limits, adding institutional weight to the AI oversight debate.

Top Stories

1. OpenAI Contractors Read Real ChatGPT Conversations

Under a program called Project Lily, OpenAI has enlisted hundreds of contractors to read and evaluate actual ChatGPT user conversations as part of ongoing model training and quality efforts. The practice has not been clearly disclosed to users, raising questions about the scope of data access and the adequacy of existing privacy policies. The story arrives alongside a separate legal dispute in which Apple alleges former engineer Chang Liu stole confidential circuit schematics and transferred them to OpenAI, with claims evidence destruction was requested. A 6-3 US Supreme Court ruling requiring warrants for location and phone data held by companies like Google and Apple adds further legal backdrop to the week's privacy-heavy news cycle.

Source: TechCrunch AI

2. OpenAI's Codex Takes Over as Company-Wide Tool

Codex and ChatGPT Work have become the operational backbone at OpenAI, with non-engineering teams reaching 90% adoption within four months of the desktop app's release. Traditional IDE usage has declined since January as Codex agents take over primary development workflows, a shift that may preview broader changes across the software industry. The resulting 10x increase in pull request volume is forcing OpenAI to rethink code review processes entirely, with automated agents now handling review, deployment, and performance monitoring. Domain experts from non-technical teams are being embedded with engineering groups to ensure AI-generated outputs meet quality standards in specialized areas.

Source: The Rundown AI

3. Dario Amodei Proposes Embedded Third-Party AI Evaluators

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has published a detailed governance proposal calling for embedded independent evaluators at AI labs, coordinated industry safety standards, and eventually binding international limits on frontier AI development. The proposal is notable for its specificity, moving beyond general advocacy to outline operational mechanisms for oversight. China's government responded to broader AI governance discussions by rejecting threat narratives and calling for open, inclusive development frameworks. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang separately affirmed he will not slow AI development, aligning publicly with the Trump administration's position at the All-In Summit.

Source: NVIDIA AI Blog
