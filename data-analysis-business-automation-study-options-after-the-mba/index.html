Data analysis and business automation: what to study after an MBA

This survey ranks eight of twenty-eight further-study options in Ireland against a single brief: business automation with Microsoft 365, and analysing data without coding. The ranking follows fit rather than price, and two of the eight cost nothing. What could not be confirmed is set out with the same care as what could.

01

Introduction to Power BI, then IT Specialist in Data Analytics

eCollege · funded by SOLAS
Free5 wk, then 16 OnlineCertiport / Cenit No NFQ level

Start here, because it costs nothing and it is exactly what you asked for.

"We work with learning providers to bring you free, part-time online programmes." Software is included, and "If your course is accredited, the cost of the online exam and certification is included in your course." Eligibility is one line: "This course is available to all adults over the age of 18 resident in the Republic of Ireland." No deadline, no application maze. If Power BI turns out not to suit you, you have lost five weeks and no money.

The honest limit: industry certificates, not NFQ awards. No ECTS, and they stack toward nothing academic.

Literally the course you named.

05

Certificate in Big Data and AI in Business: Strategy and Emerging Trends

National College of Ireland
€260 subsidised €1,300 standardL9 · 10 ECTS Thu 18:00 to 21:00Apply by 31 Aug

By far the best value per credit, if the subsidy applies to you and the coding does not put you off.

Verified on the Irish Register of Qualifications as a Level 9 Minor award worth 10 ECTS. Trinity charges €2,000 for a 5-ECTS equivalent, roughly fifteen times the cost per credit. One Saturday on campus, 17 October. Strong on the judgement half: problem framing, testing model assumptions, ethical governance including the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, and communicating results.

See the next section on SQL and Python. Note also that NCI publishes three different start dates across three of its own pages, and the practical deadline is the refund cut-off rather than the application date: "No refund will be processed once the student commences the course the week beginning 21st September."

06

Postgraduate Certificate in Artificial Intelligence for Professionals

University of Galway
€5,050 €3,095 itag Skillnet L9 Major · 30 ECTS No deadlineOnline, part time

The strongest academic award here for a non-technical manager, and the only one with no deadline pressure at all.

"For this programme, there is no specific closing date for receipt of applications. Applications will be accepted on a rolling basis." Entry is "a Level 8 honours undergraduate degree in any discipline" and itag states "no technical experience required". Three times the credit of the NCI certificates, and listed by Galway as a Major award rather than a minor one, which is a different class of qualification.

The most expensive per credit of the university options, and the page contradicts itself on duration, giving both "1 year, part-time" and "over 9 months". The itag rate requires membership, and itag notes candidates "must be EU/EEA nationals or working in Ireland on an Irish Employment Permit".

07

Certificate in AI Technologies

National College of Ireland
€300 subsidised €1,500 standardL9 · 10 ECTS Tue 18:00 to 22:00 Not yet on the register

A broad survey of what the technologies are and where they apply, rather than a course where you build things.

Credits "may be recognised" toward NCI's Postgraduate Diploma or MSc in AI for Business. Read that hedge as written, and see the evidence section on what stacking is actually worth to someone who already holds a master's.

Disclosed because you should know it: NCI advertises this as "a NFQ Level 9 Minor award (10 ECTS), accredited by QQI", and it does not currently appear in the Irish Register of Qualifications, while the Big Data certificate above does. 08

Certificate in Applied Artificial Intelligence

Dundalk Institute of Technology
Free L9 · 30 creditsOnline, part time 8 placesComputing entry bar

Free, and three times the credit of the NCI certificates. Worth one phone call on those numbers alone.

The fees field reads simply "Free", funded through the South East Cross Border Alliance under PEACEPLUS rather than the national subsidy. Modules are Statistics, Programming for Data Analytics, and Machine Learning and AI with Industrial Applications.

Two things to weigh. Capacity is listed as 8. And entry asks for "Competency in Computing (Databases, Programming and/or Data Analytics tools/platforms)", which is the opposite of "without coding". An RPL route exists if you can evidence it another way.

The stated interest was data analysis without coding. NCI's Big Data certificate, the cheapest accredited option here and the strongest on paper, builds "practical competence in interpreting large datasets using tools like SQL and Python (Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn)".

That is coding. It is taught step by step, supported by AI tools, and NCI states no prior technical background is required, so it is a gentle on-ramp rather than a computer science course. But it is not quite what you asked for, and you should know that before paying for it. If "without coding" was firm rather than loose, options 01 to 04 serve you better than 05 and 07, despite costing more or carrying no academic credit.

You already hold a master's. That changes what a short credential is worth, and the evidence is more discouraging than anyone selling a course will tell you. I would rather you saw it.

"We also find that the effect of microcredentials is lower for more experienced workers, which suggests that signaling by microcredentials and other forms of verified information are partial substitutes." And: "This effect is not driven by increased worker productivity but by decreased employer uncertainty."

Kässi and Lehdonvirta · Journal of Human Resources 59(4) · 2024

Read the mechanism, because it is the whole point. A short credential works by reducing an employer's uncertainty about someone they do not know. Your MBA and your work history already do that. You would be buying a signal you have largely already bought.

  • Short credentials pay far less than full qualifications. Singapore's own evaluation found a full qualification produced a 5.8% real wage increase, while a Statement of Attainment for individual modules produced 0.8%. A 10-ECTS certificate is the second kind.
  • Returns shrink with experience. Early-career workers saw a 6.1% premium from a first job-relevant credential; experienced workers 2.8%. Accumulation returns for experienced workers are "close to zero". Brookings Working Paper 196, 2025, descriptive rather than causal.
  • Stacking rarely happens. Between 3 and 5% of the college-educated hold stacked credentials; one administrative study found 7.8% of a graduate cohort. A 2017 review found "there is no evidence that stacking yields earnings gains"; a 2022 causal study found ~4%.
  • The regulator warns about the stacking pitch by name. QQI: "Over hyping stackability is a risk... There is a potential risk to learners who primarily aim to achieve a major qualification such as a degree, when, in the hope of building credit towards a degree, they invest time in a micro-credential that makes vague claims about being stackable."
  • And credit mostly does not travel. QQI on credit accumulation pathways: "This is likely only possible within an institution." NCI's own wording confines it to "the Postgraduate Diploma in AI for Business or the MSc in AI for Business at NCI".
  • At your level, skill retains the reward, not the paper. "higher-level degree holders retain their rewards despite their diminishing scarcity as long as they possess high skills." European Sociological Review 38(6), 2022.

What follows from that

Buy the capability, not the certificate. The evidence says the certificate will do little for you specifically. It says nothing against the skill. Power BI competence, the ability to automate a workflow, the judgement to know when an analysis is wrong: all worth having regardless of what is printed at the end.

Which is an argument for spending nothing first. Option 01 costs zero and answers the same question as option 04 at €1,695. If you finish it and still want academic credit, option 05 will run again, and the version of you who takes it will get more out of it.

The counter-argument, stated fairly: one large study found micro-credentials "display a distinctive complementarity with formal education", associated with 4.22 percentage points higher returns among graduates, and OECD reports short credentials "appear to work well when 'topping-up' existing higher education". Both are descriptive rather than causal, and the degree interaction is with a bachelor's, not a master's. No study anywhere estimates the return for a master's holder specifically. That gap is the most honest thing on this page.

Neither takes long, and both are worth doing before you pay anything. Only the paid, accredited options are affected. The free routes in 01 and 02 are open to anyone resident here.

Check 01 · Which fee applies to you

The subsidy is the difference between €260 and €1,300

The NCI prices are subsidised at 80% under the Micro-credential Learner Fee Subsidy 2026. Qualifying needs a PPS number, full-time residence in the Republic, EU, EEA, UK or Swiss nationality, and ordinary residence in that area for three of the last five years. Time held on a Stamp 2 student visa does not count toward that three years, which catches a lot of people out.

One rule you might have heard about does not apply to you. The one-year wait is keyed to a full-time undergraduate Level 8 degree: "a period of at least one year must have elapsed since an applicant completed and graduated from a full-time undergraduate degree course leading to a major award at level 8". Your MBA is a Level 9, and the same FAQ adds "I already have a higher education qualification. Am I eligible... In general, yes." The college makes the final call, so it is worth confirming.

Call NCI · 1800 221 721, option 4 springboard@ncirl.ie

Check 02 · Enrolment conditions on your permission

If you are not an EU citizen, confirm what your permission allows

Irish immigration permissions carry conditions, and some of them touch study. If you came here to study and now hold Stamp 1G, the published position is that "your other permissions and conditions remain the same as for Stamp 2", and the Stamp 2 conditions are written around full-time enrolment: "Part-time or distance learning courses do not qualify for student permission."

Nothing published speaks to the situation of someone who has finished their course, moved to Stamp 1G, and now wants to add a part-time one. It may well be fine. It is simply not written down, so it is worth a short email to Immigration Service Delivery about your own permission before you commit money to a paid course. A holder of Stamp 4 has no published study restriction at all.

This does not affect the free routes at 01 and 02, which ask only that you are over 18 and resident in the Republic.

Funding routes that do not apply, checked so you do not have to

  • Tax relief: no. Section 473A requires a postgraduate course to "last at least one academic year". A single semester falls short. NCI's entry on Revenue's approved list for 2026/27 runs to 40 programmes, all Higher Diplomas, Postgraduate Diplomas or Masters, with no Certificate or Minor award among them.
  • SUSI: no, twice over. Postgraduate support requires a full-time course of at least a year leading to a major award, and the 2026 Student Grant Scheme excludes anyone who "already holds a postgraduate higher education and training award at or above... Level 9".
  • Back to Education Allowance: no, four ways. It excludes part-time or evening courses, minor awards under 30 ECTS or 18 weeks, single modules, and single semesters.
  • Part-Time Education Option: lets a jobseeker keep their payment while studying part time, but pays nothing toward fees. "This scheme does not involve any special payment."
  • Your employer, possibly. EU AI Act literacy duties have applied since February 2025, with enforcement from August 2026. The European Commission is explicit that "There is no need for a certificate", so an employer has a duty to train and no particular reason to buy an accredited award. Still worth asking.

This list is longer than most of the findings, which is how an honest page looks. If a number matters to your decision and it appears here, get it from the source before relying on it.

  • Whether funded places remain on any NCI course. No page publishes a count, and one of the four has already filled. Only NCI can tell you.
  • Whether a Stamp 1G holder may enrol part time. Not addressed anywhere in published Immigration Service guidance. Silence, not a prohibition, and not a permission either.
  • The PL-300 exam price. Microsoft publishes no figure server-side. I will not repeat the number that circulates.
  • Why the AI Technologies certificate is absent from the Irish Register of Qualifications while its sibling is listed.
  • The fee for TU Dublin's "AI for Business Professionals", Level 9, 10 ECTS, the closest like-for-like competitor to the NCI certificates, whose page could not be located.
  • Alumni discounts. Irish business schools often discount further study for their own graduates. Nobody checked whether your MBA institution does. Ask them; it is the one place you already have a relationship.
  • Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate pricing and duration. Not retrieved.
  • Whether a spring 2027 intake exists. No NCI page publishes one, and the subsidy is stated to run "only up until December 2026". A 2027 scheme is plausible but unannounced.
  • Start dates. NCI publishes three different sets across its booklet, the Springboard portal and its own registration page. Confirm before arranging anything.
