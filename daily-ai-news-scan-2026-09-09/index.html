OpenAI says 10,000 AI agents solved a $1M math problem

Daily AI News Scan — Wednesday, September 9, 2026

Executive Summary

OpenAI's deployment of roughly 10,000 AI agents to crack the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem in 88 hours marks a significant inflection point for agentic AI at scale, drawing immediate pushback from researchers at NYU and Anthropic. On the hardware front, TSMC and Samsung both committed to ASML's High NA EUV lithography, setting the roadmap for next-generation AI chip density through the early 2030s. Google DeepMind added a scientific milestone of its own, releasing AlphaGenome Atlas—a free database mapping predicted effects for all 9 billion possible single-letter changes in the human genome.

Top Stories

1. OpenAI says 10,000 AI agents solved a $1M math problem

OpenAI deployed approximately 10,000 AI agents running on GPT-6 Astra that collectively resolved the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem—one of mathematics' most enduring open challenges—in roughly 88 hours, with formal verification led in part by researchers Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge. The result has drawn controversy from researchers at NYU and Anthropic, raising questions about the rigor of the proof and the role of human oversight in agent-generated mathematical work. The episode also surfaces broader engineering challenges: pull request volumes on GitHub have grown fivefold over three years as AI agents now generate the majority of code, forcing companies like Uber to build custom review pipelines to filter signal from noise. OpenAI is expected to announce Managed Agents at DevDay 2026, targeting businesses with advanced models and computer-use capabilities in a move that mirrors Anthropic's existing offerings.

Source: arXiv cs.LG

2. TSMC and Samsung committed to ASML's High NA EUV technology

TSMC and Samsung have both formally committed to ASML's High NA EUV lithography platform, which enables the smaller, denser chip geometries required for next-generation AI accelerators. TSMC's roadmap targets high-volume manufacturing using the technology in 2030, with a transition to larger photomasks by 2033—a shift that will require retooling across the supply chain. Samsung simultaneously secured a $200 billion partnership with Broadcom covering advanced AI chip manufacturing and HBM memory through 2030. The dual commitments effectively consolidate the industry's trajectory around ASML's platform and put further pressure on Intel to clarify its own High NA adoption timeline.

Source: The Neuron

3. Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome Atlas

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome Atlas, a free database built on the AlphaGenome model that predicts the molecular-level effects of all 9 billion possible single-letter substitutions in the human genome. The resource is available to academic researchers without cost, positioning it as a foundational reference tool for genomics, rare disease research, and drug target identification. The release follows DeepMind's pattern of productizing its scientific models—AlphaFold being the most prominent precedent—into open, queryable databases that accelerate downstream research. The scale of the predictions (covering every possible point mutation across the entire genome) represents a qualitative step beyond prior variant-effect databases.

Source: Google DeepMind Blog
