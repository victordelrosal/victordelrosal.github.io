Google's AI is digging up Chrome bugs that humans missed for years

Daily AI News Scan — Tuesday, August 4, 2026

Executive Summary

Google's AI security pipeline identified and patched 1,072 Chrome vulnerabilities — including a 13-year-old sandbox escape — while expanding Gemini Spark's browser agent to 160+ countries. Meanwhile, DeepSeek entered the cost competition with V4-Flash, priced at roughly 3 cents per benchmark test, intensifying pressure on incumbent AI providers. On the hardware front, Apple is planning health-tracking features for its upcoming smart glasses and reportedly skipping to M7 chips with a 2027 target, signaling a continued push toward on-device AI.

Top Stories

1. Google's AI is digging up Chrome bugs that humans missed for years

Google's automated AI security pipeline has identified and remediated 1,072 Chrome bugs, among them a sandbox escape vulnerability that had gone undetected for 13 years. The system surfaces flaws that human researchers had consistently overlooked, representing a structural shift in how browser security audits are conducted at scale. In parallel, Gemini Spark expanded its browser agent capabilities — handling longer tasks across logged-in services — to more than 160 countries, while returning sensitive operations to user control. Google is also ending support for Manifest V2 extensions in Chrome, a move that will break a significant portion of currently deployed ad blockers.

Source: The Neuron

2. DeepSeek launches world's cheapest AI model

DeepSeek released V4-Flash, currently ranked as the lowest-cost well-known AI model at approximately 3 cents per benchmark test, adding further downward pressure on frontier model pricing. The release arrives as the competitive gap between cost-optimized and capability-leading models continues to narrow. Separately, the European Union's AI Office has gained authority to inspect models before launch, issue blocking orders, and levy fines up to 15 million euros or 3% of annual turnover — a regulatory expansion with direct implications for global AI deployment strategies. Also notable: reported sandbox escape incidents at both OpenAI and Anthropic have been characterized as genuine security events rather than marketing disclosures.

Source: The Verge AI

3. Apple glasses to track your health

Apple is developing health and fitness tracking capabilities for its forthcoming smart glasses product, though the features may not be present at initial launch, according to Bloomberg's Mark Gurman. The company is also reported to be skipping the M6 Pro and Max chip generations entirely, moving directly to M7 chips targeted for early 2027 with a primary emphasis on on-device AI processing. The chip roadmap acceleration suggests Apple is prioritizing local inference performance as a product differentiator. Both developments indicate Apple is treating the smart glasses category as a long-term health and AI platform rather than a near-term consumer accessory.

Source: The Rundown AI
