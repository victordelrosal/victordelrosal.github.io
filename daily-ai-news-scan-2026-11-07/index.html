Tesla to end Cybertruck production by mid-2027

Daily AI News Scan — Saturday, November 7, 2026

Executive Summary

Tesla will halt Cybertruck manufacturing by mid-2027 to redirect factory capacity toward its Cybercab robotaxi, as the electric truck falls well short of sales expectations at roughly 71,000 units. Meanwhile, Mira Murati's Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Small, claiming the top spot among Western open-weight AI models, while Anthropic expanded Claude's capabilities with a beta personal finance feature allowing US users to link bank accounts directly to the assistant.

Top Stories

1. Tesla to end Cybertruck production by mid-2027

Elon Musk confirmed Tesla will cease Cybertruck production by mid-2027, reallocating manufacturing space to the Cybercab robotaxi program. The Cybertruck has sold approximately 71,000 units since its late 2023 launch, a figure that underperformed expectations for a vehicle that faced years of delays. Musk also elaborated on the Optimus humanoid robot program, describing a training methodology analogous to child development, with 10,000–20,000 robots slated to train at a dedicated facility called the Optimus Academy. The pivot signals Tesla's strategic bet that autonomous mobility and robotics carry greater long-term value than its polarizing electric pickup.

Source: The Verge AI

2. Thinking Machines claims top open-weight AI model

Thinking Machines, the AI lab founded by former OpenAI chief Mira Murati, released Inkling 2 Small, a mixture-of-experts model the company claims is the strongest Western open-weight AI model currently available. The release positions Thinking Machines as a credible competitor in the increasingly contested open-weight model space, where performance benchmarks carry significant weight for enterprise adoption. Separately, Lilian Weng, a prominent AI safety researcher, departed Thinking Machines citing health effects from workplace stress and subsequently rejoined OpenAI. The personnel movement underscores the sustained pressure inside frontier AI startups operating at this pace of development.

Source: VentureBeat AI

3. Claude can now read your bank accounts

Anthropic launched a beta personal finance feature for Claude subscribers in the United States, enabling users to link their bank accounts and query spending patterns, budgets, and financial management questions directly within the assistant. The integration moves Claude into a category of sensitive financial data that raises distinct considerations around privacy, data security, and regulatory compliance. It also reflects a broader industry trend of AI assistants seeking deeper integration with real-world personal data to increase utility and retention. The feature remains in beta, suggesting Anthropic is monitoring adoption and risk before a broader rollout.

Source: Anthropic News
