OpenAI Launches Images 2.6 with GIF Generation and Faster Processing

Daily AI News Scan — Thursday, November 5, 2026

Executive Summary

OpenAI dominated today's news cycle with the release of Images 2.6 and a confirmed acquisition of code-hosting startup Pierre Computer, while also claiming ChatGPT now handles roughly 20% of search-like queries. Google drew attention for reversing course on AI in schools, making Gemini and related tools opt-in by default following educator pushback. Meanwhile, Apple Intelligence entered China in developer beta, marking a significant regulatory and market milestone for the company's AI rollout.

Top Stories

1. OpenAI Launches Images 2.6 with GIF Generation and Faster Processing

OpenAI released Images 2.6, delivering 50% faster image generation, GIF creation, saved character references, and improved editing tools across ChatGPT, Codex, and the API. Alongside the product update, OpenAI confirmed its acquisition of Pierre Computer, a code-hosting startup that underpins Codex's repositories, signaling a move to build infrastructure that competes more directly with GitHub. Separately, OpenAI executive Adam Fry stated that ChatGPT now handles approximately 20% of search-like queries, a figure that, if accurate, represents a material challenge to Google's core business and significantly outpaces Microsoft's Bing. Taken together, these developments reflect OpenAI's accelerating push across image generation, developer tooling, and information retrieval.

Source: OpenAI Blog

2. Google Makes Gemini AI Features Optional by Default in Schools

Following backlash from educators and parents, Google reversed its approach to AI in K-12 schools, making Gemini, NotebookLM, and AI-powered Search off by default rather than bundled as active features. Schools can now enable each tool individually, giving administrators granular control over what AI students access. The policy shift highlights a persistent tension in edtech: institutions face pressure to adopt AI tools but also bear responsibility for student data privacy and appropriate use. The episode underscores how enterprise and institutional AI rollouts require opt-in frameworks to avoid trust erosion.

Source: The Verge AI

3. Apple Intelligence Opens China Developer Beta

Apple launched a developer beta of Apple Intelligence in China, featuring Writing Tools, Genmoji, and a Qwen-assisted version of Siri, with a public release targeting December. The integration of Qwen, a domestic Chinese large language model, reflects Apple's strategy of partnering with locally approved AI providers to satisfy regulatory requirements in the market. Concurrently, Apple opened Siri's AI capabilities to third-party developers via a Developer Preview, enabling custom actions and skills across iOS 27.3, iPadOS 27.3, macOS 27.3, and visionOS 27.3. The dual announcements mark a significant expansion of Apple's AI ecosystem both geographically and in developer reach.

Source: The Neuron
