Anthropic Consulting Religious Scholars on AI Ethics

Daily AI News Scan — Monday, October 5, 2026

Executive Summary

Anthropic's engagement with Catholic, Jewish, and Sikh scholars to shape Claude's moral reasoning marks an unusual intersection of theology and AI alignment — drawing direct pushback from OpenAI's Sam Altman, who warned against assigning religious authority to AI models. Meanwhile, Google's AI Overviews have now measurably reduced publisher referral traffic by 22% since their May 2024 launch, accelerating tensions across the media industry. On the policy front, the Trump administration formalized AI governance with a new task force led by AI czar Jay Clayton.

Top Stories

1. Anthropic Consulting Religious Scholars on AI Ethics

Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah spent approximately a year working with scholars from Catholic, Jewish, and Sikh traditions to inform how Claude distinguishes right from wrong — including exploring a Catholic-style confession mechanism as a model for AI accountability. The effort reflects Anthropic's broader alignment research strategy, which increasingly draws on humanistic and philosophical frameworks rather than purely technical ones. Separately, Anthropic researchers deployed Claude to compute a complex nine-loop amplitude in physics, reporting results comparable to human expert attempts but with greater efficiency. The company also opened a plugin directory submission portal for developers on paid Claude plans, expanding its ecosystem.

Source: Google News: Generative AI Enterprise

2. Google AI Overviews Cause 22% Drop in Publisher Traffic

Since Google launched AI Overviews in May 2024, referral traffic to major news publishers has declined by an average of 22%, with smaller publishers facing disproportionately severe impact. The data, reported by The Wall Street Journal, is prompting both Reddit and large media organizations to reassess content licensing agreements with Google — deals that were predicated on traffic reciprocity that no longer materializes at prior levels. The shift illustrates a structural tension at the core of the AI-search economy: Google trains on and summarizes publisher content while increasingly displacing the click-through behavior that sustains that content's economics. The renegotiation of these agreements could reshape how AI companies access web-scale training and retrieval data.

Source: The Neuron

3. Trump Named AI Czar Jay Clayton and Created Super Intelligence Force

President Trump appointed Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton as the administration's AI czar, tasking him with leading a new interagency body called the Super Intelligence Force. The task force draws participation from the FTC, Pentagon, and Office of Personnel Management, and is mandated to coordinate government engagement with AI companies and deliver a formal report on AI's risks and benefits within 120 days. The appointment formalizes what had been a fragmented federal approach to AI governance and signals the administration's intent to treat AI development as a national security-adjacent priority. Clayton's intelligence background suggests the task force's framing will lean heavily toward strategic and security considerations.

Source: The Neuron
