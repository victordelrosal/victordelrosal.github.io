Trump considers possible government stake in frontier AI labs like OpenAI or Anthropic

Daily AI News Scan — Saturday, October 3, 2026

Executive Summary

The week's dominant story is the U.S. government's deepening involvement in frontier AI: President Trump floated an Intel-style government stake in OpenAI or Anthropic, while the FTC launched formal investigations into both companies over autonomous agent risks, and a voluntary safety pledge was signed by the industry's most prominent leaders. Google countered with the release of Gemini 4 Argon, its first next-generation frontier model. Meanwhile, OpenAI expanded its platform ambitions with Dots autonomous agents and a virtual try-on feature for ChatGPT, signaling a broader push into consumer and enterprise markets.

Top Stories

1. Trump considers possible government stake in frontier AI labs like OpenAI or Anthropic

President Trump told TIME he is open to an Intel-style government equity stake in OpenAI or Anthropic, though no formal deal or timeline has been announced. Concurrently, the FTC confirmed it has opened formal investigations into OpenAI, Anthropic, and METR over consumer risks and autonomous AI agent incidents, with the probe originating in summer 2026. On the regulatory front, Trump also released a one-page voluntary safety agreement signed by executives from Google, Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, xAI, and Nvidia, committing to four layers of safety checks including independent audits and board oversight. Separately, Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5 with improved coding and agent performance at up to 30% lower cost, while NVIDIA announced a $150 billion stock buyback and SpaceXAI reported rapid GPU capacity expansion to 1.44 million total units.

Source: NVIDIA AI Blog

2. Google announces Gemini 4 Argon frontier model with early tester access

Google introduced Gemini 4 Argon, the first model in its Gemini 4 series, currently available to early testers with API pricing set at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. The model targets sustained reasoning across software engineering, enterprise knowledge work, and cybersecurity, with initial limited access granted to trusted cyber defenders. The release positions Google directly against OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra in the frontier model tier. Alongside this, Meta launched its Muse AI agent for small businesses and a broader Enterprise Platform, while OpenAI debuted Dots, its always-on autonomous agent product, as a competing personal assistant offering.

Source: TechCrunch AI

3. ChatGPT can now virtually try on clothes

OpenAI rolled out a virtual try-on feature within ChatGPT, allowing users to upload photos and preview how clothes and accessories would look on them. The announcement came alongside OpenAI's developer conference, where the company also unveiled Dots autonomous agents integrating with over 4,000 apps, GPT-6.1 Sol at one-fifth the cost of GPT-6 Astra, the Agents API in public beta, Sign in with ChatGPT authentication, and an OpenAI Marketplace — collectively framing ChatGPT as an agent operating system. Florida's attorney general separately requested an emergency court injunction to halt new OpenAI model development pending independent safety safeguards, adding regulatory pressure to the company's rapid product expansion.

Source: The Verge AI
