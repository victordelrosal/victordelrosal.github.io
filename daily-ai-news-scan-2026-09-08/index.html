OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra aces South Korea's CSAT exam with perfect score

Daily AI News Scan — Tuesday, September 8, 2026

Executive Summary

GPT-6 Astra achieved a perfect score on South Korea's rigorous CSAT college entrance exam across every subject without internet access, marking a significant frontier AI benchmark. Meanwhile, Anthropic walked away from a $6 billion acquisition of chip-efficiency startup Decart, leaving its M&A ambitions in question. On the autonomy front, Travis Kalanick re-enters the self-driving space with his startup Atoms, backed by $1.7 billion from a16z, Uber, and Bain Capital — signaling continued capital concentration in robotaxi development.

Top Stories

1. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra aces South Korea's CSAT exam with perfect score

GPT-6 Astra scored a perfect 450 on South Korea's CSAT — a college entrance exam known for breadth and difficulty — becoming the first AI model to ace every subject without internet access and while consuming fewer tokens than competing models. The model also demonstrated applied physical reasoning, successfully completing robotic manipulation tasks such as block placement and puzzle insertions, and cleared the video game Portal, illustrating advanced spatial and causal problem-solving. However, scrutiny surrounds the AGI framing: OpenAI's reported 99.9% score on ARC-AGI-3 relied on custom software scaffolding, with the base model scoring 62.7% under standard benchmark conditions. OpenAI's chief scientist Jakub Pachocki separately published an essay urging voluntary pauses among AI labs, warning that the industry is not prepared for the pace of machine intelligence development.

Source: Google News: Frontier AI

2. Anthropic drops $6 billion acquisition plan for AI startup Decart

Anthropic has pulled out of negotiations to acquire Decart, a startup focused on chip efficiency, in what would have been a deal valued at approximately $6 billion. The withdrawal marks a notable retreat for Anthropic, which had been engaged in what would have been its largest acquisition to date. The two companies may continue collaborating in a non-acquisition capacity, though no formal arrangement has been confirmed. The collapse raises questions about Anthropic's infrastructure strategy at a time when compute efficiency is increasingly central to competitive positioning among frontier AI labs.

Source: Google News: AI Startups

3. Uber co-founder returns to robotaxi race

Travis Kalanick, who built and was forced out of Uber, is re-entering autonomous transportation through his startup Atoms, which has raised $1.7 billion from a16z, Uber, and Bain Capital. The company plans aggressive hiring and acquisitions to construct self-driving technology from the ground up. Atoms is also associated with Anthony Levandowski and Pronto, bringing together figures with deep — and in some cases legally fraught — histories in autonomous vehicle development. The raise reflects sustained investor appetite for robotaxi infrastructure despite years of delayed timelines across the sector.

Source: The Rundown AI
