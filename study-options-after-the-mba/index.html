Eight options, ranked

What to study after the MBA

You asked about business automation with Microsoft 365, and about analysing data without coding. Everything below is ranked against that, not against what is cheapest or most academic. Two of the eight cost nothing. Every figure was checked against the live page on 24 August 2026, and everything I could not confirm is listed near the bottom rather than quietly left out.

Before the list

Two things that outrank every option here

Both are quick. Neither is something I can answer for you, and both change the answer completely.

Check this first if you are not an EU citizen

You may not be permitted to enrol at all

If you came to Ireland to study and are now on Stamp 1G, the Immigration Service says: "While on a Stamp 1G, your other permissions and conditions remain the same as for Stamp 2." Stamp 2 conditions say, twice: "Part-time or distance learning courses do not qualify for student permission" and "Can I change to a part-time course? No."

Nothing published addresses your exact situation, someone who finished their course, moved to Stamp 1G, and now wants to study part time. That silence is why this is a gate and not a footnote. Worth noticing: for the other kind of Stamp 1G, held by a spouse of a Critical Skills permit holder, the conditions expressly include "Permitted to undertake courses of study in the State". That line is absent from the graduate version.

Get an answer in writing from Immigration Service Delivery about your own permission before you apply for anything on this page. Do not rely on a website, including this one.

Check this second

Whether the subsidy applies decides €260 against €1,300

The NCI prices below are 80% subsidised under the Micro-credential Learner Fee Subsidy 2026. To qualify you need a PPS number, to be living full time in the Republic, to be an EU, EEA, UK or Swiss national, and to have been ordinarily resident in that area for three of the last five years. Time held on a Stamp 2 student visa counts for nothing toward that.

One rule people worry about does not apply to you. The one-year waiting period is keyed to a full-time undergraduate Level 8 degree: "a period of at least one year must have elapsed since an applicant completed and graduated from a full-time undergraduate degree course leading to a major award at level 8". Your MBA is a Level 9. The FAQ adds "I already have a higher education qualification. Am I eligible... In general, yes." Eligibility is still determined by the institution, so confirm it.

NCI 1800 221 721, option 4 springboard@ncirl.ie

Worth saying plainly

Two of the courses being recommended to you teach coding

You asked for data analysis without coding. NCI's Big Data certificate, which is the cheapest accredited option here and the one I would otherwise push hardest, builds "practical competence in interpreting large datasets using tools like SQL and Python (Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn)".

That is coding. It is taught step by step, supported by AI tools, and NCI states no prior technical background is required, so it is a gentle on-ramp rather than a computer science course. But it is not the thing you asked for, and you should know that before paying for it. If "without coding" was a firm preference rather than a loose one, options 1 to 4 serve you better than 5 and 7, despite costing more or carrying no academic credit.

The uncomfortable part

What the research says about buying any of this

You already hold a master's. That changes what a short credential is worth to you, and the evidence is more discouraging than anyone selling a course will tell you. I would rather you saw it.

"We also find that the effect of microcredentials is lower for more experienced workers, which suggests that signaling by microcredentials and other forms of verified information are partial substitutes." And: "This effect is not driven by increased worker productivity but by decreased employer uncertainty."

Kässi and Lehdonvirta, Journal of Human Resources 59(4), 2024

Read the mechanism, because it is the whole point. A short credential works by reducing an employer's uncertainty about someone they do not know. Your MBA and your work history already do that. You would be buying a signal you have largely already bought.

  • Short credentials pay far less than full qualifications. Singapore's own evaluation found a full qualification produced a 5.8% real wage increase, while a Statement of Attainment for individual modules produced 0.8%. A 10-ECTS certificate is the second kind.
  • Returns shrink with experience. Early-career workers saw a 6.1% premium from a first job-relevant credential; experienced workers 2.8%. Accumulation returns for experienced workers are "close to zero". (Brookings Working Paper 196, 2025; descriptive, not causal.)
  • Stacking rarely happens. Between 3 and 5% of the college-educated hold stacked credentials, and one administrative study found 7.8% of a graduate cohort. A 2017 review found "there is no evidence that stacking yields earnings gains"; a 2022 causal study found about 4%.
  • The regulator warns about the stacking pitch specifically. QQI: "Over hyping stackability is a risk... There is a potential risk to learners who primarily aim to achieve a major qualification such as a degree, when, in the hope of building credit towards a degree, they invest time in a micro-credential that makes vague claims about being stackable."
  • And credit mostly does not travel. QQI again, on credit accumulation pathways: "This is likely only possible within an institution." NCI's own wording confines it: credit toward "the Postgraduate Diploma in AI for Business or the MSc in AI for Business at NCI".
  • At your level, skill retains the reward, not the paper. "higher-level degree holders retain their rewards despite their diminishing scarcity as long as they possess high skills." (European Sociological Review 38(6), 2022.)

The honest conclusion

Buy the capability, not the certificate. The evidence says the certificate will do little for you specifically. It says nothing against the skill. Power BI competence, the ability to automate a workflow, the judgement to know when an analysis is wrong: all worth having regardless of what is printed at the end.

Which is an argument for spending nothing first. Option 1 costs zero and answers the same question as option 4 at €1,695. If you finish it and still want academic credit, option 5 will run again, and the version of you who takes it will get more out of it.

The counter-argument, stated fairly: one large study found micro-credentials "display a distinctive complementarity with formal education", associated with 4.22 percentage points higher returns among graduates, and OECD reports short credentials "appear to work well when 'topping-up' existing higher education". Both are descriptive rather than causal, and the degree interaction is with a bachelor's, not a master's. And no study anywhere estimates the return for a master's holder specifically. That gap is the most honest thing on this page.

Funding routes that do not apply, checked so you do not have to

  • Tax relief: no. Relief under Section 473A requires a postgraduate course to "last at least one academic year". A single semester fails. NCI's entry on Revenue's approved list for 2026/27 runs to 40 programmes, all Higher Diplomas, Postgraduate Diplomas or Masters, with no Certificate or Minor award among them.
  • SUSI: no, twice over. Postgraduate support requires a full-time course of at least a year leading to a major award, and the 2026 Student Grant Scheme disqualifies anyone who "already holds a postgraduate higher education and training award at or above... Level 9".
  • Back to Education Allowance: no, four ways. It excludes part-time or evening courses, minor awards under 30 ECTS or 18 weeks, single modules, and single semesters.
  • Part-Time Education Option: lets a jobseeker keep their payment while studying part time, but pays nothing toward fees. "This scheme does not involve any special payment."
  • Your employer, possibly. EU AI Act obligations on AI literacy have applied since February 2025, with enforcement from August 2026. But the European Commission is explicit that "There is no need for a certificate" and the Act "does not entail an obligation to measure the knowledge of AI of employees", so an employer has a duty to train and no particular reason to buy an accredited award. Still worth asking.
Honesty section

What I could not confirm

This list is longer than most of the findings, which is how it should look. If a number matters to your decision and it appears here, get it from the source before relying on it.

  • Whether funded places remain on any NCI course. No page publishes a count, and one of the four has already filled. Only NCI can tell you.
  • Whether a Stamp 1G holder may enrol part time. Not addressed anywhere in published Immigration Service guidance. Silence, not permission.
  • The PL-300 exam price. Microsoft publishes no figure server-side. I will not repeat the number that circulates.
  • Why the AI Technologies certificate is absent from the Irish Register of Qualifications while its sibling is listed.
  • The fee for TU Dublin's "AI for Business Professionals", Level 9, 10 ECTS, the closest direct competitor to the NCI certificates, whose course page could not be located.
  • Alumni discounts. Irish business schools often discount further study for their own graduates. Nobody checked whether your MBA institution does. Ask them; it is the one place you already have a relationship.
  • Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate pricing and duration. Not retrieved.
  • Whether a spring 2027 intake exists. No NCI page publishes one, and the subsidy is stated to run "only up until December 2026". A 2027 scheme is plausible but unannounced.
  • Start dates. NCI publishes three different sets across its booklet, the Springboard portal and its own registration page. Confirm before arranging anything around them.
