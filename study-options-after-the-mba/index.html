The structural difference

One of these sits on the framework and one does not

This is the fact that decides most of it, and it comes from UCD rather than from me.

"With their tight focus and duration, UCD Professional Academy Diplomas and Certificates are stand-alone certifications and are not referenced to NFQ (National Framework of Qualifications) levels or UCD University academic courses. They are not generally accepted for transfer credit onto academic courses, but are widely recognised and valued by employers."

UCD Professional Academy, Certifications and Governance

So a UCD Professional Academy award does not carry NFQ credit that transfers into a degree. That is not a criticism, and UCD states it plainly. They also state the decision rule better than I could:

"Professional Academy Diplomas are suitable for career minded learners wishing to advance their professional skills and prospects rather than their academic credentials."

UCD Professional Academy

Read that as the fork. Skills and prospects, and UCD is a fair choice at a fair price. Academic credentials that stack towards something later, and the NCI awards carry ECTS at the same level as the master's you have just finished.

Worth knowing, either way

UCD and NCI both use the word Diploma differently to each other, and UCD Professional Academy is a different thing from UCD Innovation Academy, which does run credit bearing Level 9 awards. If someone tells you UCD does an accredited diploma, that may be what they mean.

An opinion, clearly labelled

Everything else on this page is a checked figure. This paragraph is not, it is a view, and you should weigh it as one. "Without coding" used to be the whole selling point, because it meant you could do the analysis despite not being able to program. The layer that removes the coding is now the model itself: you describe the analysis and it writes and runs it. So the part that is getting scarcer is not operating the tool, it is framing the right question and noticing when the answer is wrong. You have just spent a year being trained in exactly that. It is an argument about emphasis and not a reason to skip Power BI, which people are genuinely hired for.

Before you pay anything

Three things to settle first

Each of these changes the answer, and two of them are quick phone calls rather than decisions.

  1. Are you actually eligible for the subsidy? This is the whole price question and it is worth confirming rather than assuming. If you came to Ireland on a student visa, time on that stamp does not count towards the residency requirement, and your price is the full fee rather than the subsidised one. NCI admissions will tell you in one call. Do that before anything else on this page matters.
  2. Do you need this to count towards something later? If there is any chance of further accredited study, ECTS at Level 9 are worth real money to you later and a non-framework certificate is a dead end academically. If you want a skill in hand and a recognised line on a CV by Christmas, that constraint disappears and the UCD courses compete properly.
  3. Do you want to do the work or direct it? Building the dashboard and automating the workflow is one kind of course. Judging whether an analysis can be trusted, and answering for it, is another. MBA graduates often reach for the first when the role they are about to be hired into will pay them for the second.

Things I am not certain of, told to you as uncertain

  • The full unsubsidised fees. €1,300, €1,500 and €3,000 came from NCI's own non-funded application pages, which sit behind a login I cannot show you. Each is exactly five times the student contribution, which is what an 80% subsidy produces, so they are consistent. Confirm the number with NCI before you rely on it.
  • The non-EU residency rules. The published criteria I could confirm name Stamp 4 or Stamp 4 EU FAM, refugee status or humanitarian leave to remain, plus residence for three of the last five years. I have seen other stamp categories quoted and could not verify them. If you are in this position, get it from HEA or NCI directly and not from me.
  • Whether places remain. Both listings show a future deadline and a live apply button with no "full" notice, but neither publishes a count, and one of the four courses has already filled its funded places. Apply early rather than on the 31st.
  • UCD calendar start dates and the total contact hours for the Power Automate course. Neither page states them in a way I could read.

Where sources disagree

NCI's printed 2026 micro-credentials booklet is behind its live pages in three places: it gives 22 September for AI Technologies where the portal says 29 September, 14 September for Big Data and AI in Business where the portal's date field says 17 September, and 19 October for the face-to-face Saturday where the portal says 17 October. The live figures are used throughout this page. Every NCI page also says schedules are indicative and can change, so confirm before you arrange anything around them.

One naming point that will save you a confusing phone call: these courses are funded by the Micro-credential Learner Fee Subsidy 2026, and both listings say explicitly that this is not a Springboard+ funded course, even though you apply through the Springboard+ portal and the eligibility criteria are the same.
